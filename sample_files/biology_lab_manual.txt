SECTION 1 — BIOSAFETY LEVELS AND GENERAL RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1.1  This laboratory operates at Biosafety Level 2 (BSL-2).
     Permitted organisms include: bacteria (non-pathogenic strains), cell cultures,
     fungi, and viruses that are not classified as high-risk agents.

1.2  General rules:
     - No eating, drinking, smoking, or applying cosmetics in the laboratory.
     - Mouth pipetting is absolutely prohibited — use mechanical pipette aids only.
     - Hands must be washed with antimicrobial soap after every glove removal and
       before leaving the laboratory, even if gloves were worn.
     - Lab coats must be removed before leaving the laboratory area.
     - Contaminated materials must never leave the laboratory without decontamination.

1.3  Access: Only trained and authorised personnel may enter BSL-2 areas.
     The laboratory door must remain closed during all work with biological materials.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 2 — PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

2.1  MANDATORY PPE at all times in BSL-2 areas:
     - Full-length laboratory coat (fastened at all times)
     - Nitrile gloves — change gloves between different sample types
     - Safety glasses or goggles when working with any liquid cultures or reagents

2.2  ADDITIONAL PPE required for:
     - Aerosolising procedures (vortexing, sonication, centrifugation of open tubes):
       use a face shield or surgical mask in addition to goggles
     - Working with human blood, tissue, or body fluids:
       double nitrile gloves + face shield mandatory
     - Cryogenic materials (liquid nitrogen):
       cryogenic gloves + face shield

2.3  PPE must NOT be worn outside the laboratory (corridors, offices, cafeteria).

SECTION 3 — HANDLING BIOLOGICAL MATERIALS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

3.1  GENERAL HANDLING:
     - All procedures likely to generate aerosols or droplets must be performed
       inside a Class II Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC).
     - Keep all biological material containers capped or covered when not in use.
     - Never leave cultures unattended and open on the bench.

3.2  BIOLOGICAL SAFETY CABINET (BSC) USE:
     - Allow the BSC to run for 10 minutes before starting work.
     - The BSC sash must be set to the operating height mark (approximately 20 cm).
     - Do not block the front grille — maintain at least 15 cm clearance.
     - Decontaminate the BSC interior with 70% ethanol before and after use.
     - Never use a BSC with a damaged HEPA filter or broken UV light.

3.3  PIPETTING:
     - Use only mechanical pipette controllers — mouth pipetting is NEVER permitted.
     - Use filter-tipped pipette tips when working with biological agents to prevent
       aerosol contamination of the pipette barrel.
     - Do not recap used pipette tips by hand — use one-hand technique or sharps bins.

3.4  CENTRIFUGATION:
     - Only sealed, aerosol-tight rotors and tubes may be used for biological material.
     - Tubes must be balanced to within ±0.2 g before running.
     - Inspect all tubes for cracks or leaks before loading.
     - If a tube breaks during centrifugation: do not open for 10 minutes; notify
       the supervisor; decontaminate the centrifuge with appropriate disinfectant.

3.5  SHARPS:
     - Used needles must NOT be recapped by hand.
     - All sharps (needles, scalpel blades, broken glass) go directly into a rigid,
       puncture-resistant yellow sharps container.
     - Sharps containers must be closed and replaced when they reach the fill line.

SECTION 4 — DECONTAMINATION AND STERILISATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

4.1  ALL biological waste (cultures, contaminated consumables, pipette tips) must be
     autoclaved or chemically decontaminated before disposal.

4.2  AUTOCLAVE:
     - Only trained and authorised personnel may operate the autoclave.
     - Never autoclave sealed or tightly-capped containers — pressure explosion risk.
     - Use autoclave bags and tape — do not overfill bags (≤ 75% capacity).
     - Allow autoclave loads to cool before handling — use heat-resistant gloves.
     - Log every autoclave cycle in the autoclave record book.

4.3  CHEMICAL DECONTAMINATION:
     - Standard disinfectant: 10% bleach (sodium hypochlorite) — 30 min contact time.
     - Alternative: 70% ethanol for surface decontamination (bench, BSC).
     - Liquid biological waste: add bleach to 10% final concentration, let stand 30 min,
       then dispose to drain with large water flush.

4.4  BENCH DECONTAMINATION:
     - Decontaminate bench surfaces at the START and END of each session with 70% ethanol
       or 1% Virkon solution.
     - Immediately decontaminate any spills of biological material.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 5 — STORAGE OF BIOLOGICAL MATERIALS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

5.1  Biological materials must be stored in dedicated, labelled containers:
     - Label must include: material name, risk group, date, researcher name.
     - Unlabelled biological material must be autoclaved and discarded.

5.2  Storage temperatures:
     - Live cultures and cell lines: 37°C incubator (CO₂ maintained at 5%).
     - Short-term storage: 4°C refrigerator (biological materials refrigerator only —
       no food or drinks in this refrigerator).
     - Long-term storage: -80°C freezer (log all items in the freezer inventory).

5.3  Infectious materials must never be stored in domestic or communal refrigerators.

SECTION 6 — SPILLAGE AND ACCIDENT PROCEDURES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

6.1  BIOLOGICAL SPILL ON BENCH:
     1. Alert others to avoid the area.
     2. Cover the spill with paper towels.
     3. Apply 10% bleach solution over the towels and surrounding area.
     4. Leave for 20 minutes contact time.
     5. Use paper towels to wipe up, working from outside to inside.
     6. Dispose of all contaminated materials in an autoclave bag.
     7. Decontaminate the area with 70% ethanol.
     8. Report the incident to the supervisor.

6.2  BIOLOGICAL SPILL ON PERSON:
     - Remove contaminated clothing immediately.
     - Wash affected skin with soap and water for minimum 15 minutes.
     - Flush eyes at eyewash station for minimum 15 minutes if eye contact occurred.
     - Report to the supervisor and occupational health immediately.

6.3  NEEDLESTICK OR SHARPS INJURY:
     1. Do NOT squeeze the wound.
     2. Allow to bleed freely under running water for 2 minutes.
     3. Wash with soap and water.
     4. Cover with a waterproof dressing.
     5. Report immediately to the supervisor and occupational health.
     6. Document the incident (date, time, material involved, circumstances).

6.4  CENTRIFUGE ACCIDENT (broken tube):
     - Do not open the centrifuge for at least 10 minutes after it stops.
     - Notify the supervisor before opening.
     - Wear full PPE (gloves, goggles, mask) when investigating.
     - Decontaminate all surfaces with 10% bleach.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 7 — WASTE DISPOSAL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

7.1  Biological waste categories and disposal routes:
     - Liquid biological waste: bleach-treated, then drain
     - Solid biological waste (tips, tubes, gloves): autoclave bag → autoclave → clinical waste
     - Sharps: yellow sharps container → clinical waste collection
     - Non-contaminated waste: standard recycling or general waste

7.2  Autoclave bags must be sealed before transport to the autoclave.
7.3  Never place biological waste in general laboratory waste bins.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 8 — END OF SESSION CHECKLIST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

8.1  Decontaminate bench and BSC with 70% ethanol.
8.2  Dispose of all biological waste into appropriate autoclave bags or sharps containers.
8.3  Return all cultures and reagents to appropriate storage (incubator, 4°C, -80°C).
8.4  Remove gloves and wash hands thoroughly.
8.5  Remove lab coat before leaving the laboratory.
8.6  Ensure incubators are set to correct temperature.
8.7  Close and lock the laboratory door.
8.8  Report any incidents or near-misses to the supervisor before leaving.
