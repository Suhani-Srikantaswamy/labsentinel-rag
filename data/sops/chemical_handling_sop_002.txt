=============================================================================
SECTION 1: CHEMICAL HAZARD CLASSIFICATION
=============================================================================

1.1 HAZARD CATEGORIES (GHS/OSHA aligned)
  Class 1 — Explosives: Store in approved magazine; no open flame; grounding required.
  Class 2 — Flammable gases: Store in ventilated area; away from oxidisers.
  Class 3 — Flammable liquids: Approved cabinets; limit to minimum working quantity.
  Class 4 — Flammable solids: Keep dry; isolate from ignition sources.
  Class 5 — Oxidising substances: Store separate from flammables and organics.
  Class 6 — Toxic substances: Fume hood required; double gloving; emergency antidote on hand.
  Class 7 — Radioactive materials: Designated areas only; dosimetry required.
  Class 8 — Corrosives: Acid/base cabinets; acid-resistant PPE; eyewash within 10 seconds.
  Class 9 — Miscellaneous: Follow specific SDS requirements.

1.2 MINIMUM REQUIRED PPE BY CLASS
  Class 3 (Flammable liquids): Lab coat, nitrile gloves, safety goggles, no synthetic clothing
  Class 6 (Toxic): Lab coat, double nitrile or appropriate gloves, splash goggles, fume hood
  Class 8 (Corrosive): Acid-resistant apron, splash goggles, face shield for concentrations >1M

=============================================================================
SECTION 2: TRANSPORTING CHEMICALS WITHIN THE BUILDING
=============================================================================

2.1 REQUIREMENTS
  - Use a chemical transport cart with secondary containment tray for quantities > 500 mL.
  - Never carry bottles of corrosive or flammable liquids by their necks alone — use a bottle carrier.
  - Do not transport incompatible chemicals simultaneously without physical separation.
  - Elevator use: Use freight elevator; announce presence; secure load.

2.2 PROHIBITED ACTIONS DURING TRANSPORT
  - Running or walking rapidly with chemicals
  - Using personal mobile devices during transport
  - Leaving chemicals unattended in corridors

=============================================================================
SECTION 3: SPECIFIC HIGH-HAZARD CHEMICAL PROTOCOLS
=============================================================================

3.1 HYDROFLUORIC ACID (HF)
EXTREME HAZARD — Causes deep tissue damage and systemic fluoride toxicity.
  - TWO trained personnel must be present at all times.
  - Calcium gluconate antidote gel MUST be on the bench before work begins.
  - Neoprene or heavy nitrile gloves (double layered), face shield, and chemical-resistant apron required.
  - Work only in a certified HF fume hood.
  - Emergency: Apply calcium gluconate gel to affected area IMMEDIATELY; call 911.

3.2 PERCHLORIC ACID
  - Use only in designated perchloric acid fume hoods with wash-down capability.
  - Never heat above 70°C — risk of explosive perchlorate formation.
  - Do not allow contact with organic materials.

3.3 PYROPHORIC MATERIALS (e.g., n-BuLi, DIBAL-H)
  - Handle only under inert atmosphere (glove box or Schlenk line).
  - Have dry sand or dry chemical extinguisher nearby — NEVER use water.
  - Only trained personnel with documented experience may work with pyrophorics.

=============================================================================
SECTION 4: CHEMICAL INCOMPATIBILITY REFERENCE
=============================================================================

  NEVER mix or store together:
  | Chemical A            | Chemical B               | Hazard              |
  |-----------------------|--------------------------|---------------------|
  | Concentrated acids    | Bases / ammonium salts   | Violent reaction    |
  | Oxidisers (KMnO4)     | Organics / flammables    | Fire / explosion    |
  | Bleach (NaOCl)        | Ammonia / amines         | Toxic chloramines   |
  | Bleach (NaOCl)        | Acids (HCl)              | Chlorine gas        |
  | Hydrogen peroxide     | Organics / flammables    | Explosion risk      |
  | Flammables            | Open flames / sparks     | Fire / explosion    |

=============================================================================
SECTION 5: SAFETY DATA SHEET (SDS) REQUIREMENTS
=============================================================================

  - SDS must be reviewed BEFORE first use of any new chemical.
  - SDS binder must be present in the laboratory and up-to-date.
  - Electronic SDS access must be available at each workstation.
  - Key SDS sections to review: Section 2 (Hazards), Section 4 (First Aid),
    Section 7 (Handling), Section 8 (PPE), Section 10 (Reactivity).
